# The 411 on Valley Goat

*Address:* 1100 N. Mathilda Ave\
Sunnyvale, Silicon Valley\
*Website:* <https://www.valleygoatsv.com/>\
*Phone:* 408-900-9470

![valley goat host stand](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(5).jpg)

*The Vibe:* Valley Goat was named the Best “American Restaurant” in the 2025 *Metro Silicon Valley* awards. Their food is a fusion of Indian, Japanese, Latino, Middle Eastern, and Californian cuisines, often with all those influences on the same plate! That makes for an interesting and fun dining experience as you’re never completely sure what you’re going to get, but it’s nearly guaranteed that it’ll be good.

Valley Goat is the featured restaurant at [Treehouse Hotels’ Silicon Valley campus](https://www.treehousehotels.com/silicon-valley). You may think you’re in the wrong spot when pulling into the parking lot, as “Valley Goat” is only visible in fairly small letters by the valet parking circle.

![valley goat treehouse driveway](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(2).jpg)

Park anywhere in the lot and head toward the left as you enter the grounds. Soon enough, the restaurant’s main entrance will appear.

![valley goat entrance](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(3).jpg)

Inside, plants, natural fibers, and seating abound.

![valley goat inside](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(4).jpg)

Outside, they have a lovely patio that borders the Beer Garden, another eatery on the Treehouse campus.

![valley goat patio](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(6).jpg)

It will get chilly if you’re outdoors in the evening, so bring a sweater!

Valley Goat, like many contemporary American restaurants in our area, has a menu with small plates, medium-sized plates, and large entrees, often with sides available as well. For Valley Goat, those categories are “snacks,” “plates,” and “larger plates."  In practice, the best strategy at such restaurants is to share a couple small and medium plates, and an entrée, too, if appetites are up for it. That’s what we went for this evening with a group of four.

# My Take on Valley Goat

*The Food:* We started with a round of goat empanadas, which are hefty enough to cut in half. As you can imagine, goat empanadas are one of the dishes for which Valley Goat is known.

![valley goat empanada](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(11).jpg)

I found them quite delicious! I haven’t had ground goat before, but it was a hearty flavor without venturing toward the gaminess that some diners don’t enjoy. The pastry was also hearty and rich, with plenty of lamination. The lime huacatay sauce, made with a Peruvian marigold herb, added a nice acidity and greenness to the meat and pastry elements. It felt balanced by the four tastes, which is a theme that would continue through the meal.

![valley goat empanada cut](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(16).jpg)

Next dish for the table to share was the disco fries. These are part of the ever popular “smother fries, chips, or tots with sauces and toppings” food category, and it was a hefty dish for a “snack.”

![valley goat disco fries](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(14).jpg)

The sausage was on the side to accommodate our vegetarian diner. Everyone loved the disco fries! They are doused in a coconut caramel gravy that also had a surprising amount of citrus, achieving balance in a single element of the dish. The gravy is quite good, and throw in the acidity of pickled onions and jalapenos, pungency of cilantro, velvety richness of cotija, and a healthy dollop of pork sausage and you have heaven in a bite.

Sharing a portion of the disco fries would do wonders to satisfy an appetite, but we had plenty more food to come! Next out were the chicken skewers with tahini vinaigrette and tadka crunch.

![valley goat chicken skewers](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(19).jpg)

Tadka, as I have just learned, is a method of cooking aromatics in oil, which are often preserved as a flavored oil. So I think the tadka crunch here was the colored oil coating the chicken along with crispy bits of . . . lentil, maybe? The chicken and the crunchy coating tasted great, but I’m not sure the tahini vinaigrette added much, as the chicken was juicy and flavorful enough on its own. Good-sized chunks of chicken, but it felt more like a “snack” portion than a “plate.”

As you can see, the dish classifications at Valley Goat are nebulous. And many of them come out decorated with copious amount of cilantro or mint and pickled onions, like the chicken skewers and the disco fries. That totally works for me, packing more flavor into each selection, but if you’re sensitive to onions or cilantro, you may want to be aware of how plentiful they are at Valley Goat.

Also from the “plates” classification, we tried the chickpea fritters. They’re served with yogurt made from goat’s milk, an herb chutney, tamarind, and sev crunch. Yes, that’s right, another “crunch”!

![valley goat chickpea fritters](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(20).jpg)

Sev is made from chickpea noodles, so that crunch makes perfect sense in a chickpea-focused dish. Was it the same as the crunchy bits on the chicken? I don’t think so. Mystery of mysteries! The fritter plate strongly reminded us of Indian street foods, with a mixture of crunchy and creamy together. The chickpea fritters were not the falafel-like texture we expected, more of a silken tofu that is hard-pressed, coated with a thin rice flour, and fried. Very silky! They tasted nice, but we’d have preferred a grittier, drier texture. With only four small fritters served, I do think this “plate” also might be better sold as a “snack.”

Next out was the scallion frittata, which *was* a “snack.” I so love grilled scallions, and these delivered that roasted, juicy umami.

![valley goat scallion frittata](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(25).jpg)

The focaccia’s texture and flavor were both denser and heartier than expected—maybe there was whole wheat flour in the mix? The accompanying whipped feta and charred pepper chili jam were fantastic together. I could eat spoonfuls of them, never mind the bread!

We ordered a vegetarian “larger plate” as well: the wood-fired eggplant with tahini, carrot hummus, tzatziki, more pickled onions and peppers, and a delightful pepita pita bread. The scattered herb was mint this time.

![valley goat eggplant](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(21).jpg)

What a visual feast! Every bite of the pita was a delight, and I could not get enough of the carrot hummus. The tzatziki was also quality, and the sauces drizzled on the eggplant went nice with the vegetable and the mint. The eggplant could have used another ten minutes or so of roasting, as the flesh closer to skin had that “green” quality to it. I prefer my eggplant closer to fully roasted than chewy.

Did we have room for dessert? Probably not, but we had to try it anyhow! We opted for the Fruity Sundae Funday, which was two mounds of blood orange sorbet encased in a chocolate shell and tossed with a ton of fresh fruits, oats, and puffed rice.

![valley goat fruity sundae funday](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(27).jpg)

It was a vibrant finish to the meal—would recommend!

*The Drinks:* Our dining companions had the Tropic Pop and Sweet Tart mocktails.

![valley goat mocktails](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(10).jpg)

The Tropic Pop was quite sweet, with cucumber kosho (kosho is usually a fermented yuzu paste), pineapple, and Fever Tree’s yuzu lime soda. Thumbs up, but be prepared for the sweetness! The passionfruit, lime, and grapefruit soda of the Sweet Tart was better balanced, per our companions.

I opted for the Masala Fizz with toasted coconut masala, Mt. Gay black barrel rum, pandan-lime cola, and prosecco.

![valley goat cocktail](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(12).jpg)

I didn’t even register the cola when I ordered it; my brain interpreted that to mean soda, and I’m not much of a cola fan. I couldn’t taste the cola, regardless, but the pandan’s smoothness and the subtle blend of curry spices went well with the prosecco and rum. An unexpected, but great combination.

*Would I Go Back?* We truly were fascinated by the copious herb mounds and pickled onions on each dish, and we also found it slightly odd that several dishes had key elements not mentioned in the menu description. The sundae, for example, had no mention of fresh fruit at all, and the pita with the eggplant was listed simply as “pita,” not as a nut-covered feast.

<div class="caption">

[![valley goat eggplant](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(23).jpg)](https://thegourmez-wpmedia.s3.amazonaws.com/2026/10/valley-goat+(23).jpg) The pita served with the eggplant plate. </div>

The servers do ask about allergies before taking orders, so we had no surprises in that sense, but wondered what guided the decisions about which ingredients to list and which ones not to.

I think Valley Goat might also be better served combining the “snacks” and “plates” section of their menu entirely, as the differences in sizes between them were nonexistent and/or inconsistent. Also, the menu often combined ingredients as though they were one unit when listing them, which is confusing. For example, the “pineapple fever tree lime & yuzu soda” was pineapple juice AND a Fever Tree lime & yuzu soda, two separate ingredients in a mocktail.

That said, nearly every dish zestfully integrated acidity, salt, heat, bitterness, and sweetness and were clearly well prepared and well envisioned. We were excited to see what would come out next and to imagine how the listed ingredients would be presented. I’d not hesitate to return. I’d only repeat the empanadas, disco fries, and scallion focaccia, but I’d be intrigued to find out what else may surprise and delight me.
